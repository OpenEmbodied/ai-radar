# Radar · 2026-11-05

## Signals

- **The coding agent becomes the data collector.**
  - PyRUA-DataFarm: from one calibration episode, a coding agent rebuilds the task in simulation, writing the scene, an expert controller and a success judge. A video model then renders photoreal multi-view episodes from the simulator's exact actions. Policies trained only on this data match teleoperation-trained ones on five real tasks (93 vs 84 successes in 100 trials). Under an unseen tablecloth and colored light they keep 87 successes where teleoperation-trained policies fall to 46. Each task costs about $21 to adapt, and each further episode about $0.80 (first-party).
  - ARPG (NeurIPS 2026) uses LLM agents to design whole RL pipelines (MDP formulation and simulator) rather than to act at runtime.
- **Recovery and grounding become explicit harness stages.**
  - DiMuRe chooses between reusing the program, running predefined recovery skills (including bounded VLA grasping) and having an MLLM write corrective code, based on phase-specific evidence. It verifies each attempt, and reports 77.65% versus 58.68% complete-task success under mid-execution disturbances on its new LIBERO-Disturb benchmark (first-party).
  - FEIJOA grounds instructions at test time and checks preconditions and effects for every skill. On a real Spot, 17 of 35 successes came after recovering from a failed tool call or check.
- **Readable reasoning is not a safety certificate.** "Say the Same, Act Differently" (NeurIPS 2026) steers a reasoning VLA's action while keeping its rationale word-for-word identical. It causes new collisions in 52.6% of driving scenes, so a supervisor who reads the rationale would see nothing wrong.
- **Evaluation gets faster.** RoboScope Kit wraps policies and benchmarks once and schedules simulator workers and policy replicas separately, running LIBERO-Long 4.45–6.16× faster than the official evaluator with equivalent success (first-party).
