# Radar · 2026-11-18

## Signals

- **Force comes to frozen frontier models.** ForceHarness tackles two problems. Contact changes faster than an API model can decide, so self-terminating force primitives regulate force on their own and return at a contact event. A frozen model cannot read raw force streams, so a physics translation layer returns each trace as physical quantities with uncertainty. On a real arm with no wrist force sensor, GPT-6 Astra's zero-shot success rises from 3.3% to 90.0% (first-party, code released).
- **Frontier-scale backbones as policies.** ScaleVLA attaches an action DiT to a 552B DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash backbone and compares backbones from 2B to 552B under one recipe. It reports (first-party):
  - 10 demonstrations beat a 2B model trained on 50;
  - LIBERO-PRO reaches 45.1%, against at most 31.3% for smaller backbones;
  - real charger insertion goes from 4/10 to 9/10;
  - median latency is 216 ms per action chunk.
- **Agents that don't take their own word for success.** These systems separate deciding from checking:
  - A Matrix with Steps learns only from externally confirmed outcomes: a simulated robot that judged its own success solved 35% of tasks, against 79% with external verification (first-party).
  - LeaP-VLN and AgenticJevNav both add independent verifiers that gate progress and trigger recovery.
  - CAST certifies sampled action chunks against a barrier condition before choosing among them.
- **Calibration over demonstrations.** Self-Calibrating Vision-Agent-Action measures its own command map and camera geometry, and succeeds in 268 of 360 trials in changed robot cells, against 76 for the best of eight released VLAs (first-party).
- **Reward models get a reckoning.** A survey of 114 robotic reward models introduces RRM-Bench-1K. Its finding: evaluators rank whole executions well but track how far an execution has progressed more than the actual task state.
