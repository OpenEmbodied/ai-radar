# Radar · 2026-11-28

## Signals

- **No Friday listing.** arXiv skipped the 27 Nov announcement for the US Thanksgiving holiday. This run cleared the queue built up over the week's very large listings: 12 papers, all verified earlier and held back only by the daily cap.
- **Change during execution is costly, and the cost lingers.** CALVIN-MAX builds 7,828 cases, each changing one thing mid-execution: the objects, the camera, the robot or the instruction. A policy that still completes the affected instruction is 5.0 to 16.8 points less likely to complete the next one (first-party). This matches DTU-Bench and RoboLiveMem (2026-11-25 and 27). AWAIT adds tasks whose action windows open and close in time, where agents struggle to recognize when a window has opened.
- **Consolidation and calibration.**
  - Robo-MOPD distills several specialist robot planners into one 9B generalist that calls tools, reaching 72.9% on LIBERO-PRO and 44.8% on RoboCasa365 (first-party).
  - "Calibrating World Action Models" gives a frozen world-action model a calibrated confidence signal, hands control to an agent only when confidence drops, and later distills those takeovers back into the model.
  - A capability-contract interface lets damaged quadrupeds decline far fewer missions at a fixed risk limit: about 7% to 8% versus 32% (first-party).
- **Infrastructure and benchmarks.**
  - MotionServe shares one H200 across 96 simulated robots by scheduling around each robot's execution slack, cutting stalls by 43.4% (first-party).
  - RoboPRO measures clutter and unsafe contacts; WorldPhy scores the fine-grained physics of world-model videos.
  - MRBench asks which robots a task actually needs.
  - Atomic Policy and Real2Worlds2Real round out the agentic-harness additions.
