## Signals

- **Self-improving agents can learn the benchmark's checker instead of the task.** "Learning the Task or Learning the Verifier?" studies what happens when a self-improving coding agent can see the benchmark's success checks. Scores rise quickly, but the gain disappears when the checks change slightly while the instructions stay the same, and the resulting skills transfer poorly. The paper proposes success criteria that follow the instruction rather than the original check. RoboARCade finds frontier agents falsely claim success on 24–39% of subtasks (first-party). Both matter for the steady stream of self-improvement results.
- **Self-improvement keeps reaching real robots and leaderboards.**
  - PyRUA-REAL: coding agents turn real-world exploration into persistent memory and reusable executable skills that can later run without an online agent. It reports 91.1% over eight real tasks and 84.4% on 42 RoboDojo tasks (first-party).
  - Compounding Physical RSI: traces failures to the stage where progress breaks, refines the policy with offline RL, and gates each generation on retaining earlier skills. It reports first place on the RoboDojo-Sim leaderboard (first-party).
- **Harnesses for whole bodies.** HITCH gives a VLM a humanoid harness: pick a point in the image to get its 3D position, then command pelvis, torso, hands, walking and gaze. A VLM fine-tuned on teacher trajectories through the harness completes 51% of SIMPLE episodes and carries a box on a real G1 without real training data (first-party).
- **Agentic-robotics benchmarks multiply.** RoboARCade evaluates frontier agents as direct robot policies across three embodiments; GPT-6 Astra scores 49.8%. VABAR provides industrial and medical task variation on Newton, with 33 programmatic reference skills. RoboCasa-Mem separates environment memory from process memory.
